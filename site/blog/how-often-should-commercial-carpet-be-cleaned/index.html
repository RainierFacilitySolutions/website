How often should commercial carpet be cleaned? A guide by facility type and traffic level

Commercial carpet needs deep cleaning every 12-18 months baseline, but high-traffic areas need extraction every 4-6 months. Learn how often your facility type should be professionally cleaned.

By Mike Bennett, Content Writer · November 23, 2026

High-traffic areas like medical waiting rooms and retail floors often need extraction every 4 to 6 months, while low-traffic private offices may stretch to 18 months. Carpet manufacturer warranties often set minimum cleaning intervals and may require CRI Seal of Approval products.

Introduction

If you manage a commercial building, you've likely wondered whether your carpet cleaning schedule matches what your carpet actually needs. The answer isn't a one-size-fits-all calendar date. Medical waiting rooms, retail floors, schools, and private offices accumulate soil at very different rates, and each has its own standards for sanitation and appearance. This guide breaks down how often commercial carpet should be cleaned by facility type, explains why fixed schedules fail to protect your investment, and shows you how to build a cleaning program that fits your building's real traffic and your warranty requirements.

You'll learn what the Carpet and Rug Institute and carpet manufacturers recommend, how Pacific Northwest weather affects drying time and soil tracking, and why a layered approach (daily vacuuming, interim cleaning, and periodic deep extraction) works better than extraction alone. Whether you're a facility manager, office manager, or property owner in Pierce, King, or Snohomish County, this guide will help you set a cleaning frequency that keeps your carpet looking sharp, lasts longer, and meets your insurance and warranty terms.

Carpet manufacturers take cleaning frequency seriously because it directly affects warranty coverage. Shaw requires professional hot water extraction at least yearly, and Mohawk requires it every 18 months, with CRI Seal of Approval products. Skipping or delaying deep cleaning may put your warranty coverage at risk, so confirm the exact terms in your manufacturer's warranty documents.

The industry standard comes from the Carpet and Rug Institute (CRI), which recommends professional deep cleaning every 12 to 18 months, adjusted for foot traffic, industry type and carpet material. But this is a floor, not a ceiling. High-traffic facilities like medical offices, schools, and retail stores often need extraction every 4 to 6 months, with interim maintenance every 2 to 4 weeks in between. Low-traffic private offices may stretch to 18 months between deep cleans if daily vacuuming and interim cleaning stay on track.

The difference comes down to soil load. When people walk across carpet, they track in dirt, dust, allergens, and moisture, especially in the Pacific Northwest, where wet weather and outdoor grime are the norm from fall through spring. Regular vacuuming pulls surface dirt, but only hot-water extraction removes embedded grit, oils, and microbes that build up in the carpet pile and backing over time. That grit acts like sandpaper on carpet fibers, accelerating wear long before the carpet's actual lifespan runs out. A layered maintenance program, walk-off mats at entries, daily HEPA vacuuming, interim encapsulation cleaning, and periodic hot-water extraction, stops that cycle and keeps your carpet investment protected.

The sections below walk you through the cleaning needs of each facility type, show why generic schedules fail, and explain how to build a program that actually works for your building's traffic, your warranty, and your budget.

The short answer: It depends on traffic, not a calendar

According to the Carpet and Rug Institute, professional deep cleaning should happen every 12 to 18 months as a baseline. However, this interval is a starting point, not a rule. High-traffic areas like medical waiting rooms, retail showrooms, and school hallways need extraction every 4 to 6 months to manage soil, allergen buildup, and spills. Meanwhile, a low-traffic private office might safely stretch to 18 months if it's isolated from main walkways and receives regular vacuuming.

Carpet manufacturer warranties set minimum cleaning schedules. According to a vendor summary, Shaw requires professional hot water extraction at least annually, while Mohawk requires it every 18 months, with CRI Seal of Approval products. Skipping these intervals may jeopardize your warranty, so confirm the terms in your manufacturer's own documents before setting your schedule.

The EPA also emphasizes that frequently used areas should be cleaned more often than rarely used ones, and advises that carpet should not be cleaned unless it can dry within 24 hours. This timing constraint matters especially in the Pacific Northwest, where wet seasons and high humidity can extend drying time. After-hours or overnight extraction scheduling is common precisely because it allows carpet to dry before the next business day.

How often should commercial carpet be cleaned by facility type

Carpet cleaning needs vary dramatically by how the space is used. A medical waiting room and a corporate back office in the same building may need entirely different extraction schedules, so breaking frequency down by facility type gives you a realistic roadmap.

Medical and dental practices: Waiting rooms, treatment areas, and common spaces see constant foot traffic, spills, and tracked-in soil. Patients sit in waiting areas, often with wet shoes in winter. Heavy-traffic zones like these are often put on professional extraction every 4 to 6 months to keep carpet clean and looking good. Lower-traffic staff offices or storage rooms separate from patient areas can usually wait longer.

Offices and coworking spaces: Hallways and common areas in office buildings experience moderate, predictable traffic. Hallways and common areas in office buildings see moderate traffic, so the right interval falls between the heavy-traffic and low-traffic ranges and depends on how many people move through daily. Private offices away from main walkways may go 12 to 18 months between deep cleans. Kitchens and break rooms often need extraction on the shorter end of the range because spills are frequent.

Retail and restaurants: Retail floors, restaurant dining areas, and customer-facing zones accumulate soil, tracked-in grit, and spills constantly. Extraction every 4 to 6 months is a common suggestion for heavy-traffic carpet like this. Food and beverage spills can also set stains and odors if not cleaned promptly, so high-impact zones may need extraction on a quarterly schedule.

Schools and childcare centers: Frequent spills, allergens, and tracked-in soil from multiple daily entries put schools in the heavy-traffic category, where extraction every 4 to 6 months is a common suggestion. Children's classrooms, cafeterias, and playrooms generate constant soiling. Daily vacuuming with a high-filtration vacuum and walk-off mats at every entrance reduce the soil load between extractions. Scheduling extraction during breaks, when the building is less occupied, also helps carpet dry.

Why one-size-fits-all cleaning schedules fail

A fixed schedule treats every area of a building the same, which creates two problems: either you over-clean low-traffic zones and waste money, or you under-clean high-traffic areas and let soil accumulate faster than you can reverse it.

Traffic patterns within a single building vary enormously. A lobby may accumulate tracked-in soil and mud daily and need extraction at the shorter end of the heavy-traffic range, while a back-office hallway with light foot traffic might stretch to 18 months. If you schedule the whole building on one frequency, you're guessing. A layered maintenance program catches this variation. Daily vacuuming with a high-filtration vacuum removes surface soil and allergens. Interim cleaning between deep extractions, often every 2 to 4 weeks in heavy-traffic areas, helps keep carpet fresh between hot water extractions. Periodic hot water extraction removes ground-in soil and embedded particles that vacuuming alone cannot pull out.

Pacific Northwest weather also breaks one-size-fits-all logic. Wet seasons track in mud, grit, and moisture that embeds in carpet fibers. Scheduling extraction after hours or on weekends allows carpet to dry before staff and customers return, and the EPA advises against cleaning carpet that cannot dry within 24 hours.

Inspection-based adjustments are the real advantage of a professional carpet care program. When supervisors walk through your building weekly and note traffic patterns, spills, and soil levels, they can flag when a zone needs earlier extraction or when a quieter area can safely wait longer. This beats a static calendar. Many commercial facility managers build carpet care into their broader janitorial contract and let their cleaning team track and recommend adjustments based on what they see on the ground.

The role of daily care in extending extraction intervals

Daily vacuuming with a high-filtration vacuum is the foundation of any carpet maintenance program. It removes surface grit, allergens, dust and soil that act like sandpaper on carpet fibers, though only hot-water extraction pulls out what is embedded deeper in the pile. In high-traffic areas like entryways, hallways and waiting rooms, daily vacuuming prevents soil from being ground deeper into the pile, which means extraction intervals can be extended and carpet life is preserved.

Walk-off mats at every entrance capture moisture and tracked-in soil before they reach interior carpet. This single step cuts the amount of soil your extraction crew has to remove per visit, which directly reduces how often you need restorative cleaning. During the Pacific Northwest's wet season, when tracked-in grit and moisture spike, entrance mats become critical to carpet longevity.

Skipping extraction to save money can shorten carpet life. Embedded grit acts as an abrasive that wears carpet fibers like sandpaper. Manufacturer care instructions spell this out: Shaw requires professional hot water extraction at least yearly, and Mohawk requires it every 18 months, with CRI Seal of Approval products. Regular vacuuming and proper interim care between extractions help keep carpet in good condition.

A layered approach works best: daily HEPA vacuuming in high-traffic zones, entrance mats to cut soil at the source, and periodic hot water extraction on a schedule tied to traffic and facility type. Your cleaning team's supervisor walkthroughs are your early warning system; they catch soil buildup and spills before they set, so you're maintaining rather than recovering.

Setting a carpet cleaning schedule that fits your building

Start by mapping your facility's traffic patterns, not by picking a calendar date. High-traffic zones (lobbies, hallways, waiting areas in medical and dental practices, retail floors) warrant extraction every 4 to 6 months or sooner, while low-traffic areas like private offices or storage may safely extend to 12 to 18 months between visits.

Your carpet manufacturer's recommendations are non-negotiable if you want to honor warranty terms. Follow manufacturer maintenance recommendations alongside regular vacuuming and periodic wet extraction cleaning to keep your carpet performing. An IICRC-certified firm can assess your specific carpet, your facility's use patterns, and your climate to recommend a realistic plan. Build carpet care into your janitorial contract rather than treating it as a separate vendor. When the same team handles daily vacuuming and periodic extraction, supervisor walkthroughs catch soil levels and performance dips early. You adjust the schedule based on what's actually happening in your building, not on guesswork. For example, our crews track traffic patterns and spill activity during their weekly walkthrough visits, then recommend extraction timing that matches your building's real needs.

Drying time is crucial to your schedule, especially in the Pacific Northwest. Hot water extraction typically requires 4 to 12 hours for carpet to dry depending on humidity and ventilation, so scheduling extraction after hours or on weekends ensures carpet is ready before staff and customers arrive. Dry time varies with humidity and ventilation, so factor in extra drying time when conditions are damp and plan extraction accordingly.

Document your schedule in writing so it's clear to your team and to any visiting clients or inspectors. If you manage multiple facilities across Tacoma, Kent, Renton or beyond, one point of contact who coordinates all your carpet care keeps the standard consistent across buildings.

Conclusion

The right frequency for commercial carpet cleaning is not a fixed number. It depends on how your building is used, how much soil enters from outside, and what your carpet manufacturer requires to honor the warranty. Daily vacuuming with high-filtration equipment, entrance mats to control soil at the source, and periodic hot water extraction timed to your traffic patterns form a complete program that extends carpet life and keeps your facility looking professional year-round.

The trap of one-size-fits-all schedules is they either over-clean and waste money or under-clean and let your carpet deteriorate. An IICRC-certified cleaning firm that inspects your building weekly and adjusts the extraction schedule based on what they see beats any fixed calendar. In the Pacific Northwest, where moisture and tracked-in soil are seasonal challenges, this inspection-based approach is especially valuable.

Key takeaways:

  • High-traffic commercial spaces need extraction every 4 to 6 months; low-traffic areas may extend to 12 to 18 months between deep cleans.
  • Daily HEPA vacuuming and entrance mats prevent soil buildup and can reduce extraction frequency.
  • Follow your carpet manufacturer's care instructions to protect your warranty and extend carpet life.
  • Schedule extraction after hours or weekends so carpet dries before business hours.
  • Hire an IICRC-certified firm to assess your building and recommend a schedule based on actual traffic, not a calendar.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my carpet look dirtier after professional cleaning?

A common cause is cleaning solution left behind after extraction. Rainier's hot-water extraction process includes a low-residue rinse for this reason. If carpet looks dirtier after a cleaning, ask your provider to inspect it and re-clean the affected areas.

What is the lifespan of commercial carpet?

Commercial carpet lifespan depends on traffic, material, and care. Regular vacuuming, timely extraction, and drying carpet promptly help extend its life, while neglected carpet wears faster as embedded grit acts like sandpaper on fibers.

Is carpet outdated in 2026?

No. Carpet remains a common choice in offices, medical practices, schools, and retail because it absorbs sound and provides comfort underfoot. The right flooring depends on your building's use, traffic, and maintenance plan.

What is the difference between hot water extraction and encapsulation cleaning?

Hot water extraction (also called steam cleaning) pre-treats, agitates, and rinses carpet deeply to remove soil, allergens, and stains. Encapsulation uses a low-moisture cleaning agent that dries quickly and can be done between extractions to extend intervals. Extraction is restorative; encapsulation is interim maintenance.

Do I need to follow my carpet manufacturer's cleaning schedule to keep the warranty valid?

Check your warranty terms. According to a vendor summary, Shaw requires professional hot water extraction at least yearly and Mohawk every 18 months, with CRI Seal of Approval products. Confirm the exact requirements in your manufacturer's own documents before scheduling.

How do I know if my carpet needs professional cleaning before the scheduled date?

Signs include visible stains that don't lift with spot treatment, a musty or stale odor, discoloration in high-traffic areas, or increased dust and allergens visible in the air. High-traffic zones like entryways and waiting rooms may need extraction sooner than planned if soil visibly accumulates faster than expected.

What should I do if my carpet gets water damage?

The EPA advises that water-damaged carpet be thoroughly cleaned and dried within 24 hours of the damage, or removed and replaced, to avoid mold and bacteria. For active water damage, call a professional right away.
